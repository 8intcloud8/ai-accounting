# Proactive Australian tax review

Apply this workflow during transaction-use and classification reviews. Read `official-sources.md` and verify applicable current official guidance; the links below are discovery seeds, not a substitute for reading sources.

## Decide before asking

1. Reuse confirmed entity, period, GST registration/basis, transaction purpose, documents and prior applicable user rules. Inspect available evidence first. Do not ask the user to choose a tax rule or repeat a supplied fact.
2. Assess separately: accounting movement/category; business/private use; income-tax deductibility; GST on the supply; entitlement to a GST credit; attribution timing. A business payment is not necessarily deductible or GST-creditable.
3. Determine and explain the recommended treatment from the evidence and official rule. Record exact source, relevant paragraph/section, applicability to this entity/period and access date in the supporting workpaper. Keep the visible review concise; do not reintroduce an Evidence column.
4. Separate a rule decision from a missing amount/document. State precisely what remains unresolved and why. A missing loan principal/interest split must not leave known zero GST pending. An invoice needed to measure a credit is not a reason to ask whether the general rule exists.
5. Ask only a fact that could change treatment and cannot be established from available records, e.g. loan versus lease/hire purchase, attendees, property use, taxable component or business percentage. Group repeated fact questions. If guidance cannot be verified, follow the official-source access-failure workflow rather than asking the user to decide the law.
6. Reuse the evidence-backed result across matching transactions when facts remain applicable. Conflicting new documents require reassessment. Recommendations and user confirmations do not silently authorise journal postings.

## Cases that must not become unnecessary questions

| Established facts | Recommended review approach | Genuine remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary loan repayment | No GST credit on loan principal or interest; record known zero GST. Principal reduces liability, not expense. Assess interest separately for deductibility. | Principal/interest breakdown; whether agreement is actually a lease or hire purchase if not established. GST on the separately financed asset and separately supplied services is a different assessment. |
| Confirmed wages/salary paid to an employee | Outside GST; no GST credit. Do not leave salary GST pending for a tax invoice. | Payroll evidence, period and employee/director accounting where necessary; a salary-like bank description alone is not confirmation. |
| Verified same-owner bank transfer or credit-card debt repayment | Balance-sheet movement, not income/expense; no new GST credit on moving funds or settling debt. Keep underlying purchases and lender fees separate. | Ownership/destination if unknown; company-to-director movements need separate loan/payroll/reimbursement assessment, not an assumed own-account transfer. |
| Confirmed personal purchase | No business GST credit and no business deduction for private consumption. Do not label the supplier's actual supply GST-free merely because the claim is zero. | Company-paid private items may need director-loan/FBT assessment; preserve unresolved accounting treatment. |
| Customer restaurant dinner | Assess entertainment rules even if business was discussed. Client entertainment generally has no deduction or GST credit; assess employee/FBT components and exceptions separately. | Actual attendees, venue/context and applicable exception if material. Do not claim the dinner solely because user calls it business. |
| Confirmed business insurance | Identify insurance type and applicable rule; taxable premium components may support a credit if requirements met. Never assume total bank payment / 11: stamp duty and other components may differ. | Policy/tax invoice component amounts and creditable percentage; general insurance versus life/health or another type. |

Other cases (overseas software, refunds, mixed-use utilities, deposits and capital purchases) require their own applicable official guidance. Do not force every merchant into one of these examples or infer supplier GST registration from its name.

## Review and persistence

Keep category/use confirmation distinct from tax-rule status and amount-evidence status in supporting records. In Excel, show an accurate concise GST treatment such as `outside GST`, `no GST`, `no credit—personal`, `pending evidence` or `GST applies`; extend dropdown choices when necessary. Preserve original amounts, stable identities, prior confirmations, exclusions and existing workbook edits. Update the saved review when a rule resolves an issue; leave only the actual missing fact/document in Notes. Keep unknown amounts blank; never fabricate invoice GST or post a journal to populate a report.

Official starting points:
- [ATO financial services questions and answers](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/adhoc-sgml/gstir-financial-services-ch00.pdf), chapter 2, loans.
- [ATO TR 97/17](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TXR/TR9717/NAT/ATO/00001), food/drink entertainment; check status and relevant examples.
- [ATO GSTR 2001/3](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=GST/GSTR20013/NAT/ATO/00001), GST and fringe benefits/entertainment.
- [GST Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/latest/text), sections 9-20 (employee activities), 11-15 (creditable purpose), 69-5 (non-deductible expenses), 78-5 (insurance premiums). Check the version applicable to the reporting period.
